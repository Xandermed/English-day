🎺 San Fermín · Spain

4 presentadores · unos 5 minutos cada uno, todo en inglés. Lo que está entre corchetes es una acción (mostrar un objeto). La traducción es solo para estudiar.

Student 1 · Origin

Hello everyone! Welcome to our stand. My name is ___. Today we are going to talk about San Fermín. It is a famous festival from Spain. [Point to Spain on the map]

Spain is a country in Europe. San Fermín is celebrated in a city called Pamplona, in the north of Spain. [Point to Pamplona]

Who is San Fermín? In English, his name is Saint Fermin. He was a young religious leader, a bishop, who lived many centuries ago. He is the patron saint of Navarre, the region of Pamplona. A patron saint is a saint who protects a city or a place. [Show the picture]

Now, the history. In the Middle Ages, more than 700 years ago, Pamplona had a religious festival for Saint Fermín. It was in October, and it was cold and rainy. So, in the year 1591, the city moved the festival to July, when the weather is warm and sunny. In July, there were also markets and bull events, so all the celebrations joined together.

The festival became famous in the world because of an American writer called Ernest Hemingway. He wrote a novel about it, in 1926. After that, visitors from many countries started to come to Pamplona.

Now I will pass to my partner. Thank you!

Student 2 · How and why we celebrate

Hello! My name is ___. I am going to tell you how people celebrate San Fermín.

The festival starts on July 6, at noon, with a big moment called the chupinazo. A person shoots a rocket from the balcony of the city hall, and thousands of people in the square shout and celebrate. Everybody is wearing white and red. [Show the picture]

Every morning, at eight o'clock, there is the famous encierro. In English, it is the running of the bulls. Six bulls run through the narrow streets of the old city, from a corral to the bullring. The run is very short, only about three minutes. Some brave people run in front of the bulls. It is dangerous, and only experienced adults can do it. Some people love this tradition, and other people think it is not good for the animals. [Show the map of the route]

There is much more than the bulls. There are parades with giants and big heads. They are huge colorful figures made of paper and wood. There are bands, traditional dances and fireworks every night. [Show the giants]

On July 14, at midnight, people sing a sad song called "Pobre de mí". It means "Poor me". They take off the red scarf, because the festival is over.

Why do people celebrate? They celebrate to honor their saint, to have fun and to share their culture with the world.

Thank you! Now my partner will tell you when it is and what you need.

Traducción al español

¡Hola! Mi nombre es ___. Les voy a contar cómo celebra la gente San Fermín.

El festival empieza el 6 de julio, al mediodía, con un gran momento llamado el chupinazo. Una persona lanza un cohete desde el balcón del ayuntamiento y miles de personas en la plaza gritan y celebran. Todos visten de blanco y rojo. [Muestra la imagen]

Cada mañana, a las ocho, está el famoso encierro. En inglés es "the running of the bulls". Seis toros corren por las calles estrechas del casco antiguo, desde un corral hasta la plaza de toros. La carrera es muy corta, solo unos tres minutos. Algunas personas valientes corren delante de los toros. Es peligroso y solo adultos con experiencia pueden hacerlo. A algunas personas les encanta esta tradición y otras piensan que no es buena para los animales. [Muestra el mapa del recorrido]

Hay mucho más que los toros. Hay desfiles con gigantes y cabezudos. Son figuras enormes y coloridas hechas de papel y madera. Hay bandas, bailes tradicionales y fuegos artificiales todas las noches. [Muestra los gigantes]

El 14 de julio, a medianoche, la gente canta una canción triste llamada "Pobre de mí". Se quitan el pañuelo rojo, porque el festival terminó.

¿Por qué celebra la gente? Celebran para honrar a su santo, divertirse y compartir su cultura con el mundo.

¡Gracias! Ahora mi compañero les dirá cuándo es y qué se necesita.

Student 3 · When and what we need

Hello! My name is ___. I will tell you when San Fermín is and what you need.

First, when. San Fermín is every year from July 6 to July 14. That is nine days. In July, it is summer in Spain, and the days are long and hot. [Point to the calendar]

Now, what do you need? Let me show you. [Show each item]

Number one: white clothes. Everybody wears a white shirt and white pants. It is the traditional uniform of the festival.

Number two: a red scarf. It is called pañuelo. You wear it around your neck. You can make one with a red cloth or a red paper strip. [Show the scarf]

Number three: a red sash. It is called faja. You wear it around your waist. A red ribbon or a red cloth works very well.

Number four: a giant head. You can make a big paper head with cardboard and colors. Draw a happy face and a colorful hat. You can wear it or put it on a stick. [Show the big head]

Number five: music. In Pamplona, there are bands with trumpets and drums. You can bring a small drum or use the music from a phone.

Number six: traditional food. People eat tortilla, sandwiches and drink lemonade or juice in the street.

Thank you! Now my partner has some questions for you.

Traducción al español

¡Hola! Mi nombre es ___. Les diré cuándo es San Fermín y qué se necesita.

Primero, cuándo. San Fermín es cada año del 6 al 14 de julio. Son nueve días. En julio es verano en España y los días son largos y calurosos. [Señala el calendario]

Ahora, ¿qué se necesita? Déjenme mostrarles. [Muestra cada objeto]

Número uno: ropa blanca. Todos usan camisa blanca y pantalón blanco. Es el uniforme tradicional del festival.

Número dos: un pañuelo rojo. Se llama pañuelo. Se usa alrededor del cuello. Puedes hacer uno con una tela roja o una tira de papel rojo. [Muestra el pañuelo]

Número tres: una faja roja. Se llama faja. Se usa alrededor de la cintura. Una cinta roja o una tela roja funcionan muy bien.

Número cuatro: una cabeza gigante. Puedes hacer una cabeza de papel con cartón y colores. Dibuja una cara feliz y un sombrero colorido. Puedes ponértela o ponerla en un palo. [Muestra la cabeza grande]

Número cinco: música. En Pamplona hay bandas con trompetas y tambores. Puedes traer un tambor pequeño o usar música del celular.

Número seis: comida tradicional. La gente come tortilla, sándwiches y toma limonada o jugo en la calle.

¡Gracias! Ahora mi compañero tiene algunas preguntas para ustedes.

Student 4 · Keyword, vocabulary and questions

Hello! My name is ___. Now it is your turn to participate. First, the keyword for your Festival Passport. The keyword for our festival is SCARF. Please write it: S-C-A-R-F. Also write: Festival, San Fermín. Country, Spain.

Now let's learn five new words. Please repeat after me. [Show each picture] Scarf. Saint. Street. Giant. Fireworks. Choose one word for your passport. Write it in the box "one new word".

Let's play a game. I ask a question, and you answer.

Question one: In which country is San Fermín? Answer: Spain.

Question two: In which city is it? Answer: Pamplona.

Question three: What color is the scarf? Answer: Red.

Question four: What color are the clothes? Answer: White.

Question five: What is the name of the morning event with the bulls? Answer: The encierro, or the running of the bulls.

Question six: What do people do on July 14 at midnight? Answer: They sing "Pobre de mí" and take off the scarf.

Very good! Now a challenge. Let's dress up! [Give a red scarf to a volunteer] Who can say three things we need for San Fermín in English? For example: "A white shirt, a red scarf and a red sash."

Finally, write in your passport one thing people do at San Fermín. For example: "People watch parades" or "People wear white and red".

Let's say goodbye. In Spanish, people shout: "¡Viva San Fermín!" Everybody together: Viva San Fermín! Thank you for visiting our stand. Enjoy the next festival!
