🌸 Hanami · Japan

4 presentadores · unos 5 minutos cada uno, todo en inglés. Lo que está entre corchetes es una acción (mostrar un objeto). La traducción es solo para estudiar.

Student 1 · Origin

Hello everyone! Welcome to our stand. My name is ___. Today we are going to talk about Hanami. It is a beautiful tradition from Japan. [Point to Japan on the map]

Japan is an island country in East Asia. It has four seasons, and spring is one of the most beautiful. In spring, thousands of cherry trees have pink and white flowers. The cherry blossom is called sakura in Japanese. [Show the picture]

The word Hanami has two parts. "Hana" means flower, and "mi" means to see. So Hanami means "flower viewing". It is exactly that: people go to look at the flowers.

Now, the history. Hanami is more than 1,000 years old. At first, the nobles in the emperor's court did it. They sat under the trees, wrote poems and enjoyed the beauty of the flowers. Later, the samurai and then the common people started to do it too. Soon everyone in Japan was celebrating Hanami.

Sakura flowers are special because they are very short. The blossoms last only one or two weeks. Then the wind takes the petals away, like pink snow. For Japanese people, this is a beautiful lesson. Life is short and precious, so we need to enjoy every moment.

Now I will pass to my partner. Thank you!

Student 2 · How and why we celebrate

Hello! My name is ___. I am going to tell you how people celebrate Hanami.

First, everybody watches the news. In Japan, the weather news says when the cherry trees will bloom. There is even a "cherry blossom forecast". The flowers open first in the warm south, and then the blossom moves north, week by week. [Show the map]

When the trees bloom, families, friends and coworkers go to parks. They bring a big blue sheet or a blanket and sit under the trees. They take a picnic with them. The picnic box is called bento. It has rice, fish, eggs, vegetables and sweets. [Show the bento picture]

A famous Hanami sweet is hanami dango. It is a stick with three sweet rice balls. One is pink, one is white and one is green. People also drink green tea. They talk, laugh and listen to music. They look at the flowers and take many photos. [Show the dango picture]

At night, some parks put lanterns around the trees. The flowers look magical in the light. This is called yozakura, or night cherry blossoms.

Why do people celebrate? They celebrate to welcome spring, to enjoy nature and to spend time with people they love. They also remember that life is beautiful and short.

Thank you! Now my partner will tell you when it is and what you need.

Traducción al español

¡Hola! Mi nombre es ___. Les voy a contar cómo celebra la gente Hanami.

Primero, todos ven las noticias. En Japón, las noticias del clima dicen cuándo florecerán los cerezos. Incluso hay un "pronóstico de la flor del cerezo". Las flores se abren primero en el cálido sur y luego la floración sube hacia el norte, semana a semana. [Muestra el mapa]

Cuando los árboles florecen, las familias, los amigos y los compañeros de trabajo van a los parques. Llevan una lona azul grande o una manta y se sientan bajo los árboles. Llevan un picnic. La caja del picnic se llama bento. Tiene arroz, pescado, huevos, verduras y dulces. [Muestra la imagen del bento]

Un dulce famoso de Hanami es el hanami dango. Es un palito con tres bolitas dulces de arroz. Una es rosada, una es blanca y una es verde. La gente también toma té verde. Conversan, ríen y escuchan música. Miran las flores y toman muchas fotos. [Muestra la imagen del dango]

En la noche, algunos parques ponen faroles alrededor de los árboles. Las flores se ven mágicas con la luz. Esto se llama yozakura, o cerezos de noche.

¿Por qué celebra la gente? Celebran para dar la bienvenida a la primavera, disfrutar la naturaleza y pasar tiempo con las personas que quieren. También recuerdan que la vida es hermosa y corta.

¡Gracias! Ahora mi compañero les dirá cuándo es y qué se necesita.

Student 3 · When and what we need

Hello! My name is ___. I will tell you when Hanami is and what you need.

First, when. Hanami is every year in spring. In the city of Tokyo, the cherry trees usually bloom in late March or early April. In the north of Japan, the flowers open in May. The best time lasts only about one week. In Japan, the school year also starts in April, so cherry blossoms mean new beginnings. [Point to the calendar]

Now, what do you need? Let me show you. [Show each item]

Number one: a blanket or a blue sheet. You sit on it under the trees.

Number two: a picnic. Bring a bento box with rice, fruit and sweets. At our stand, we have a drawing of a bento. You can draw your own bento on paper.

Number three: paper cherry blossoms. Cut five round petals from pink paper. Glue them together in a flower. Add a yellow circle in the center. Then glue the flowers on a branch made of brown paper. [Show the branch]

Number four: pink and white clothes. In Japan, some people wear a light summer kimono, called a yukata. You can wear something pink or white.

Number five: green tea or juice. Bring a drink to share.

Number six: a camera or a notebook. Take a photo or write a short poem about the flowers.

Thank you! Now my partner has some questions for you.

Traducción al español

¡Hola! Mi nombre es ___. Les diré cuándo es Hanami y qué se necesita.

Primero, cuándo. Hanami es cada año en primavera. En la ciudad de Tokio, los cerezos suelen florecer a finales de marzo o principios de abril. En el norte de Japón, las flores se abren en mayo. La mejor época dura solo una semana más o menos. En Japón, el año escolar también empieza en abril, así que las flores de cerezo significan nuevos comienzos. [Señala el calendario]

Ahora, ¿qué se necesita? Déjenme mostrarles. [Muestra cada objeto]

Número uno: una manta o una lona azul. Te sientas en ella bajo los árboles.

Número dos: un picnic. Trae una caja bento con arroz, fruta y dulces. En nuestro stand tenemos un dibujo de un bento. Puedes dibujar tu propio bento en papel.

Número tres: flores de cerezo de papel. Corta cinco pétalos redondos de papel rosado. Pégalos juntos formando una flor. Agrega un círculo amarillo en el centro. Luego pega las flores en una rama hecha de papel café. [Muestra la rama]

Número cuatro: ropa rosada y blanca. En Japón, algunas personas usan un kimono ligero de verano, llamado yukata. Puedes usar algo rosado o blanco.

Número cinco: té verde o jugo. Trae una bebida para compartir.

Número seis: una cámara o un cuaderno. Toma una foto o escribe un poema corto sobre las flores.

¡Gracias! Ahora mi compañero tiene algunas preguntas para ustedes.

Student 4 · Keyword, vocabulary and questions

Hello! My name is ___. Now it is your turn to participate. First, the keyword for your Festival Passport. The keyword for our festival is SAKURA. Please write it: S-A-K-U-R-A. Also write: Festival, Hanami. Country, Japan.

Now let's learn five new words. Please repeat after me. [Show each picture] Blossom. Picnic. Petal. Spring. Beautiful. Choose one word for your passport. Write it in the box "one new word".

Let's play a game. I ask a question, and you answer.

Question one: In which country is Hanami? Answer: Japan.

Question two: What does Hanami mean? Answer: Flower viewing.

Question three: What is the cherry blossom called in Japanese? Answer: Sakura.

Question four: What do people do under the trees? Answer: They have a picnic.

Question five: What is the name of the picnic box? Answer: Bento.

Question six: How long do the flowers last? Answer: One or two weeks.

Very good! Now a challenge. Let's write a very short poem about sakura. Use three lines. For example: "Pink flowers fall. / The spring wind is soft. / I smile and look up." Work with a partner and write your own poem in English. Who wants to read it to us?

Finally, write in your passport one thing people do at Hanami. For example: "People eat under the trees" or "People look at flowers".

Let's say goodbye. In Japanese, we say "Arigato". It means "Thank you". Everybody together: Arigato! Thank you for visiting our stand. Enjoy the next festival!
